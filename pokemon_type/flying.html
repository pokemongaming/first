Flying-Type Pokémon GO Overview

Flying is one of Pokémon GO's most recognizable types. Its biggest advantage is not simply the Flying label itself: dual typing, access to Flying attacks, Ground immunity and the weather system can all affect how an individual Pokémon performs.

Trainer tip: Never evaluate a Flying Pokémon only from its type. A Flying/Dragon Pokémon, Flying/Steel Pokémon and Flying/Fighting Pokémon can have very different defensive profiles.

🪽 Flying Attacks

Flying-type attacks can be useful against Fighting, Bug and Grass opponents, making Flying attackers valuable in specific raid and PvP matchups.

⚡ Key Weaknesses

Flying Pokémon can be vulnerable to Electric, Ice and Rock attacks depending on their complete typing.

🌍 Ground Immunity

Flying typing provides an important Ground-type immunity interaction, although dual typing can alter other parts of the matchup.

Flying Type Explained

Flying is especially interesting because it appears in many dual-type combinations. Those combinations can completely change a Pokémon's weaknesses, resistances and battle role.

Flying / Normal

A common combination with a broad matchup profile. Individual species must be evaluated using their complete typing.

Flying / Dragon

Dragon/Flying Pokémon have a very different defensive profile from pure Flying Pokémon and should be analyzed separately.

Flying / Steel

Steel provides numerous additional resistances and changes the practical value of Flying's weaknesses.

Flying / Fighting

This combination can provide useful offensive coverage while creating its own set of weaknesses.

Flying-Type Weaknesses & Resistances

For a pure Flying-type Pokémon, Electric, Ice and Rock are important weaknesses. Flying also has a distinctive interaction with Ground attacks.

⚠️ Flying Weaknesses

Electric Ice Rock

These are the core weaknesses of a pure Flying-type defensive profile.

🛡️ Flying Resistances / Immunity

Ground: Immune Bug Fighting Grass

Flying provides important defensive interactions against Ground, Bug, Fighting and Grass attacks.


Dual-type warning: A Flying/Steel Pokémon does not have the same weaknesses as a Flying/Dragon or Flying/Fighting Pokémon. Individual guides should always contain the complete matchup chart.

Flying-Type Weather & Battle Conditions

Weather can influence move performance and encounter conditions in Pokémon GO. Explain weather effects alongside the specific Pokémon rather than treating every Flying Pokémon identically.

🌬️ Windy Weather

Flying-type attacks can receive weather-related benefits under appropriate in-game conditions.

⚡ Rainy Weather

❄️ Snow

Snow can strengthen Ice-type attacks, another important consideration against Flying Pokémon.

How to Choose a Flying Pokémon

1. Identify the Target

First determine whether you need a Flying Pokémon for raids, PvP, gyms, collection or another purpose.

2. Check Dual Typing

Never assume every Flying Pokémon has the same weaknesses. The second type can completely change the matchup.

3. Check the Moveset

Fast-move energy generation and charged-move efficiency can change a Pokémon's practical performance.

4. Consider Weather

Weather can affect both your Flying attacker and the counter moves used against it.

5. Compare Forms

If Mega, Shadow, Dynamax or Gigantamax versions exist, compare them separately.

6. Spend Resources Carefully

Candy, Stardust, XL Candy and Elite TMs can be limited. Explain whether the investment is actually worthwhile.

Flying-Type Pokémon GO FAQ

What is Flying weak to in Pokémon GO?

Pure Flying typing is weak to Electric, Ice and Rock attacks. Dual typing can change the complete matchup.

What types are Flying strong against?

Flying-type attacks are especially useful against Fighting, Bug and Grass-type opponents.

Does Flying have an immunity to Ground?

Flying typing has a special defensive interaction with Ground-type attacks. Individual Pokémon should still be evaluated using their complete typing and current game mechanics.

What is the best Flying Pokémon?

There is no universal answer. A raid attacker, PvP Pokémon and defensive Pokémon can have completely different requirements.

Is Rayquaza good for raids?

Rayquaza can be a major Flying/Dragon investment, but its current moveset, form, availability and the specific raid boss should be considered before recommending it.

Are all Flying Pokémon good raid attackers?

No. Type alone does not determine raid performance. Stats, moves, survivability and the boss matchup all matter.

Should Mega, Shadow and Dynamax information appear on every Flying Pokémon page?

Only when those forms actually exist and are relevant to that species. Conditional content is much better than filling every page with irrelevant sections.
