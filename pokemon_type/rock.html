Rock-Type Pokémon GO Overview

Rock is one of Pokémon GO's most useful offensive types, especially when facing Flying, Bug, Fire and Ice opponents. However, Rock Pokémon can have very different defensive profiles because many species combine Rock with a second type.

Trainer tip: Do not judge a Rock Pokémon by CP alone. Attack stats, moveset, typing, survivability and the exact raid matchup all influence its practical value.

🪨 Rock Offense

Rock attacks are particularly useful against several common raid targets, including Flying-, Bug-, Fire- and Ice-type opponents.

🌊 Important Weaknesses

Pure Rock Pokémon have important weaknesses including Water, Grass, Fighting, Ground and Steel attacks.

☀️ Weather Boost

Partly Cloudy weather is particularly important when considering Rock-type attack performance and encounter conditions.

Rock Type Mechanics

Rock Pokémon frequently appear alongside Ground, Water, Steel, Dark and other types. The second typing can greatly change how a species performs in battle.

Rock / Ground

A common combination with strong offensive possibilities but several important defensive weaknesses.

Rock / Dark

This combination creates a very different defensive profile from pure Rock Pokémon.

Rock / Steel

Steel adds substantial defensive utility but also creates new matchup considerations.

Rock / Water

Water can alter both defensive weaknesses and practical battle roles compared with pure Rock Pokémon.

For a pure Rock-type defensive profile, Water, Grass, Fighting, Ground and Steel attacks are important weaknesses. Rock also has useful resistances that can make it valuable in specific matchups.

Rock-Type Weather

⛅ Partly Cloudy

Partly Cloudy weather is especially relevant to Rock-type attacks and can make Rock counters more attractive in appropriate raid matchups.

🌧️ Rain

Rain can strengthen Water-type attacks, which is important when using Rock Pokémon against opponents carrying Water moves.

☀️ Sunny

Sunny weather can strengthen Grass and Ground attacks, two important Rock weaknesses.

How to Choose a Rock Pokémon

1. Identify the Battle

Determine whether the Pokémon is being evaluated for raids, PvP, gyms, collection or another purpose.

2. Check the Typing

Always check the second type. Rock/Ground and Rock/Dark, for example, should not be treated as identical.

3. Compare Moves

A Pokémon with excellent stats can still underperform if its available moves are poor for the intended role.

4. Consider Weather

Partly Cloudy weather is particularly relevant to Rock attacks, while other weather can strengthen its counters.

5. Check Special Forms

Compare Mega, Shadow, Dynamax and Gigantamax forms only when they actually exist for the Pokémon.

6. Consider Investment

Explain whether Stardust, Candy, XL Candy or Elite TMs are justified before recommending a power-up.

Rock-Type Pokémon GO FAQ

What is Rock weak to in Pokémon GO?

Pure Rock typing is weak to Water, Grass, Fighting, Ground and Steel attacks. Dual typing can change the complete matchup.

What is Rock strong against?

Rock-type attacks are particularly useful against Fire, Ice, Flying and Bug-type opponents.

Is Rock good for raids?

Yes, Rock can be an important raid attacking type when the boss is weak to Rock. The best Pokémon depends on the specific matchup and available moves.

What is the best Rock Pokémon?

There is no universal winner. The best choice depends on whether you are looking for raid damage, PvP utility, defense, Mega utility or another role.

Is Tyranitar a good Rock Pokémon?

Tyranitar can be an important Rock/Dark investment. Its usefulness should be evaluated using its current moves, form, stats and the battle you intend to use it in.

Is Rhyperior good for raids?

Rhyperior can have strong Rock and Ground applications. The optimal moveset depends on the raid target and the specific role you need.

Does every Rock Pokémon have a Mega Evolution?

No. Mega Evolution is species-specific. Only include Mega information where that Pokémon actually has a Mega form in Pokémon GO.

Should Shadow information be included on every Rock page?

Only when the species has a relevant Shadow form. Conditional sections are preferable to adding irrelevant content merely to make a page longer.

Editorial Policy & Data Methodology

Pokémon GO is a live-service game. Moves, availability, events, raid rotations, forms and other game mechanics can change. Rock-type pages should therefore be reviewed regularly instead of being published once and forgotten.

The purpose of this hub is to help Trainers understand Rock Pokémon rather than simply display a large collection of statistics. Individual guides should include original explanations, comparisons and practical recommendations.

Temporary information should include a "Last Checked" date. Avoid presenting an event-specific encounter as permanent availability.
