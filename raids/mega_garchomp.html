Introduction

Mega Garchomp is the mega evolved form of Garchomp, a Dragon/Ground type Pokémon known for its brutal attack power and fast-paced combat style. In its Mega form, it becomes even more aggressive, trading a bit of speed for significantly higher raw damage output. In raids, this means you’re not just fighting a tank—you’re dealing with a heavy hitter that punishes weak counters instantly.

What makes Mega Garchomp interesting is that it doesn’t feel like a “simple raid boss.” It forces coordination or strong counters because sloppy teams get wiped quickly.

Personal Raid Experience

My first Mega Garchomp raid was much tougher than I expected. I initially entered the battle with several Dragon-type attackers, thinking they would perform well. While they dealt decent damage, they also fainted quickly whenever Mega Garchomp used powerful Dragon-type attacks.

The biggest improvement came when I switched to a team focused on Ice attackers. Pokémon like Mamoswine and Glaceon consistently dealt far more damage, and the raid timer suddenly felt much less stressful. Having another trainer use a Mega Ice Pokémon also made a noticeable difference, helping the entire group defeat the boss more efficiently.

That experience reinforced an important lesson: when facing Mega Garchomp, proper counters matter far more than simply bringing your strongest Pokémon.

Who Should Raid Mega Garchomp?

Mega Garchomp is a raid worth considering for almost every type of trainer.

Newer players can use these raids as an opportunity to collect Mega Energy and add a powerful Dragon/Ground-type Pokémon to their collection. Intermediate trainers may be looking for stronger raid attackers, while experienced players often target high-IV specimens, XL Candy, or shiny encounters.

If you regularly participate in raids and enjoy building powerful teams, Mega Garchomp is one of the Mega Evolutions that can provide long-term value well beyond its initial release.

Strengths and Weaknesses Summary

Every raid boss has its advantages and drawbacks, and Mega Garchomp is no exception.

One of its biggest strengths is versatility. It performs exceptionally well as both a Dragon-type and Ground-type attacker, allowing it to remain useful across many different raid scenarios. Its Mega Evolution also provides valuable attack boosts for teammates, making it a strong choice in group content.

On the downside, obtaining enough Mega Energy for the first Mega Evolution can take several raids. It can also be challenging for smaller groups, especially if players are not using proper Ice-type counters.

Overall, the strengths far outweigh the weaknesses, which is why Mega Garchomp continues to be a highly desirable Mega Pokémon.

High IV Hunting Guide

Not every Garchomp you catch after a raid will be equal. Some will have better Individual Values (IVs), which determine how strong that Pokémon can become when fully powered up.

If you're planning to invest Stardust, Candy, and Mega Energy into Garchomp, it's worth taking the time to look for a high-IV specimen. Trainers who focus on raids often prefer stronger IVs because they provide slightly better overall performance, while Master League players usually aim for the highest IV combinations possible.

Even if your first catch isn't perfect, Mega Garchomp raids provide multiple opportunities to keep searching for a stronger one.

Should You Use Remote Raid Passes?

For many trainers, the answer is yes.

Mega Garchomp offers a combination of useful rewards, Mega Energy, shiny potential, and long-term PvE value that makes it a worthwhile target. If you still need Mega Energy or don't yet have a strong Garchomp, using a Remote Raid Pass can be a good investment.

However, if you already have plenty of Mega Energy and a well-built Garchomp, you may choose to save your passes for future raid bosses. Ultimately, the decision depends on your goals and available resources.

Mega Garchomp vs Mega Salamence

Mega Garchomp and Mega Salamence are both powerful Dragon-type Mega Evolutions, but they fill slightly different roles.

Mega Salamence is often favored when pure Dragon-type damage is the priority. Its offensive performance against Dragon-weak raid bosses is outstanding and makes it a popular choice among experienced trainers.

Mega Garchomp, on the other hand, offers greater flexibility. In addition to its Dragon-type power, it can also function as an excellent Ground-type attacker. This dual utility allows it to remain relevant in a wider variety of raid battles.

If you're looking for versatility, Mega Garchomp is often the more flexible option. If your focus is purely Dragon-type damage, Mega Salamence may have a slight edge in specific situations.

Why Mega Garchomp Remains Popular

Years after its introduction, Mega Garchomp continues to be one of the most sought-after Mega Evolutions in Pokémon GO. A large part of its popularity comes from its combination of power, versatility, and iconic design.

Whether you're raiding for Mega Energy, hunting for a shiny, building stronger teams, or simply collecting your favorite Pokémon, Mega Garchomp offers something for nearly every trainer. Few Mega Evolutions manage to stay relevant across so many different aspects of the game, which is why it remains a frequent target whenever it returns to raids.

Trivia

Mega Garchomp has always been a fan-favorite Pokémon thanks to its intimidating appearance and incredible strength. Its design is often compared to a land shark, which perfectly matches its aggressive battle style.

In the main series Pokémon games, Mega Garchomp gains a massive increase in offensive power but loses some of its Speed. This trade-off turns it into an even more destructive attacker capable of overwhelming opponents with raw force.

Garchomp was also the signature Pokémon of Cynthia, one of the most memorable Champions in Pokémon history, which helped cement its popularity among trainers.

PvP Perspective

While Mega Evolutions are not currently allowed in standard GO Battle League formats, Garchomp itself continues to be a popular choice in Master League.

Its combination of strong attacks, solid bulk, and useful typing allows it to pressure many common opponents. Players who already have a high-IV Garchomp for raids may find that it can also perform well in PvP with the right moveset.

PvE Performance

Mega Garchomp is one of the strongest Mega Evolutions available for raid battles. Its combination of Dragon and Ground-type attacks allows it to perform well against a wide variety of raid bosses.

When used as a Mega Evolution, it not only deals excellent damage but also boosts attacks of similar types for other trainers in the raid. Because of this, Mega Garchomp remains a valuable choice whenever Dragon or Ground-type damage is needed.

If you're looking to build a strong PvE roster, Mega Garchomp is definitely a Pokémon worth investing resources into.

Raid Preparation Checklist

Before challenging Mega Garchomp, it’s worth taking a few minutes to prepare your team. This raid can be surprisingly tough if you enter with random Pokémon, especially when it has powerful Dragon or Ground-type moves.

A good starting point is to build a team around strong Ice-type attackers, as they deal the most damage by far. If you have a Mega Ice Pokémon available, consider using it for the extra team-wide damage boost. It's also a smart idea to stock up on Revives and Potions beforehand since Mega Garchomp can hit hard enough to knock out several Pokémon during the battle.

If you're raiding with friends, try to coordinate your teams rather than relying on whatever the game recommends. A little preparation can make the difference between a close battle and a comfortable victory.

Mega Energy Information

Defeating Mega Garchomp rewards Mega Energy, which is needed to unlock and power up your own Mega Garchomp. The amount of Mega Energy you receive depends on how quickly your group completes the raid, so faster clears generally mean better rewards.

Once you've collected enough Mega Energy, you can Mega Evolve your Garchomp and take advantage of its impressive offensive power. Even after the initial Mega Evolution, future Mega Evolutions become much cheaper, making it a worthwhile long-term investment for trainers who enjoy raids.

Best Counters

The best strategy against Mega Garchomp is simple: bring strong Ice attackers and avoid anything that doesn’t hit its weaknesses.

Some top-performing options include:

  • Mamoswine – One of the best overall choices. It deals massive Ice-type damage and is relatively easy to build.
  • Weavile – Very fast and high damage, but a bit glassy, so it requires dodging.
  • Glaceon – A solid and reliable Ice attacker with good bulk.
  • Baxcalibur – If you have it powered up, it performs extremely well thanks to strong Ice moves.
  • Galarian Darmanitan – High damage output but very fragile, so timing matters.

Dragon and Fairy counters can work, but Ice types are simply in a different league for this raid.

Typing and Weaknesses

Mega Garchomp is a Dragon and Ground type, which gives it several key weaknesses:

  • Ice
  • Dragon
  • Fairy

The biggest thing to remember is that Ice-type damage absolutely melts Mega Garchomp. If you come prepared with strong Ice attackers, the fight becomes much easier. Without Ice Pokémon, the raid feels noticeably harder and more resource-heavy.

At the same time, it resists:

  • Fire
  • Poison
  • Rock
  • Electric

So using those types is usually inefficient and will slow down your raid progress.

Can Mega Garchomp Be Soloed, Duoed, or Trioed?

  • Solo: Not realistically possible for most players due to its high attack power and bulk.
  • Duo: Possible with high-level teams, optimized Ice counters, and good coordination.
  • Trio: Comfortable and the most practical setup for average trainers.

If you and your group have strong Ice teams, this raid becomes much more manageable, but it still punishes underprepared players.

Weather Boost Impact

Weather plays a big role in this raid:

  • Snowy weather: Boosts Ice types → makes the raid significantly easier
  • Windy weather: Boosts Dragon types → makes Mega Garchomp more dangerous
  • Sunny/Clear weather: Boosts Ground-type moves → also increases difficulty

If possible, try to raid during Snowy weather or at least neutral conditions. Windy weather should be avoided unless your team is very strong.

Mega Garchomp CP Information

When you encounter Mega Garchomp in raids, its CP can vary depending on raid tier and weather boost. After catching Garchomp (non-mega form), you can Mega Evolve it using Mega Energy.

  • High CP encounters usually indicate strong IV potential.
  • Weather boost can slightly increase catch CP.

This makes it worth checking every encounter carefully before using your berries and Poké Balls.

Shiny Availability

Yes, Garchomp can be shiny, and its shiny form carries over into its Mega evolution. The shiny version has a noticeable color difference, making it a desirable target for collectors. However, shiny odds remain the same as standard raid shiny rates, so it’s still a luck-based chase.

Is Mega Garchomp Worth Raiding?

You should raid Mega Garchomp if:

  • You need a strong Dragon/Ground mega evolution
  • You want top-tier Dragon-type raid attackers
  • You are building a strong raid team collection

It may not be the easiest mega raid, but it is absolutely worth the effort because Mega Garchomp remains useful in both raids and PvE content for a long time.

Mega Garchomp Moveset

Mega Garchomp is not just about raw stats—it’s dangerous because of its coverage moves. In raids, it can use a mix of Dragon and Ground-type attacks that punish unprepared teams.

Common moves you may encounter include:

  • Fast Moves: Dragon Tail, Mud Shot
  • Charged Moves: Earth Power, Outrage, Fire Blast

Earth Power is especially dangerous because it hits hard and can quickly take down Electric, Steel, and Fire-type Pokémon. Outrage is another high-pressure move that punishes non-Fairy or non-Ice teams.

Understanding its moveset helps you decide when to dodge and when to tank damage.

Team Size Difficulty Breakdown

Mega Garchomp’s difficulty depends heavily on trainer levels and team quality:

  • Low-level trainers: 4–5 players recommended for safety
  • Mid-level trainers: 3–4 strong players can comfortably clear it
  • High-level optimized teams: 2–3 trainers possible with strong Ice counters
  • Random public lobbies: Can be risky due to inconsistent counters

Even though it’s not the hardest Mega raid, it still punishes weak or unprepared groups quickly.

Best Party Strategy

A smart raid plan makes a big difference in this fight.

Start with your strongest Ice-type attackers, since they deal the highest damage. If possible, include a Mega Pokémon like Mega Glalie or Mega Abomasnow to boost Ice-type damage for the entire team.

Key strategy tips:

  • Lead with Ice attackers (Mamoswine, Weavile, Glaceon)
  • Save Mega Evolution for maximum team boost effect
  • Dodge heavy charged moves like Earth Power when using glass cannons
  • Re-enter quickly if your team faints early

Good coordination can turn this raid from difficult to very smooth.

Mega Bonus Strategy

Mega Evolution isn’t just about damage—it boosts your entire team.

Using Mega Ice Pokémon gives:

  • Increased Ice-type damage for all allies
  • Faster raid completion time
  • Better efficiency in energy and revive usage

Best Mega options for this raid:

  • Mega Glalie
  • Mega Abomasnow

This small detail often makes the difference between a close win and a comfortable raid.

Common Mistakes Trainers Make

Many raids fail not because Mega Garchomp is unbeatable, but because of avoidable mistakes:

  • Using Dragon-type Pokémon (they get out-damaged easily)
  • Ignoring Ice-type counters
  • Staying in during Earth Power without dodging
  • Using under-leveled Pokémon just for participation
  • Raiding in Windy weather without proper counters
  • Not relobbying when all Pokémon faint early

Avoiding these mistakes immediately improves your win rate.

Catch Tips

Once Mega Garchomp is defeated and you encounter Garchomp:

  • Use Golden Razz Berries for maximum catch rate
  • Wait for attack animation before throwing
  • Aim for Excellent Curveball throws
  • Take your time—there is no rush in raid catches

If you’re lucky, you may also encounter a shiny Garchomp, which is always a guaranteed catch if you use a berry.

FAQ

Can Mega Garchomp be soloed?

No, it is not realistically soloable due to its high stats and strong coverage moves.

What is Mega Garchomp weak to?

It is weak to Ice (4×), Dragon, and Fairy-type moves.

What is the best counter for Mega Garchomp?

Mamoswine is generally the best overall counter, followed by Weavile and Glaceon.

Is Mega Garchomp good for PvP?

Yes, it can be strong in certain PvP formats, especially Master League, due to its high attack pressure.

What weather is best for this raid?

Snowy weather is best because it boosts Ice-type damage.

Conclusion

Mega Garchomp is not a “casual” raid boss. It rewards preparation and punishes random teams. If you go in with proper Ice-type counters and a coordinated group, the fight becomes smooth and even fun. If you don’t, it quickly turns into a struggle. Think of it like this: Mega Garchomp isn’t just about winning the raid—it’s about proving your raid team is actually built for endgame.
