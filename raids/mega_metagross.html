Mega Metagross Introduction

Mega Metagross is one of the most anticipated Mega Evolutions in Pokémon GO. Known for its incredible Attack stat and excellent Steel/Psychic typing, this powerhouse can become a valuable addition to almost any trainer's roster. When Mega Metagross appears in Mega Raids, many players will want to challenge it not only for the Mega Energy but also because it has the potential to become one of the strongest Steel-type attackers in the game.

This guide covers everything you need to know before entering the raid, including weaknesses, counters, recommended group sizes, weather effects, shiny availability, and whether Mega Metagross is worth your raid passes.

Catch Tips After the Raid

Defeating Mega Metagross is only half the challenge. The catch encounter is where patience becomes important.

Using Golden Razz Berries is usually the safest strategy, especially if the Metagross has a high CP. Waiting for its attack animation before throwing can greatly improve accuracy and reduce wasted Premier Balls.

Curveball throws remain one of the easiest ways to increase catch chances. If you're comfortable landing Great or Excellent Throws consistently, take your time rather than rushing each attempt.

It's also worth checking the CP immediately after the encounter begins. High CP values can indicate strong IVs, giving you an extra reason to stay focused during the catch phase.

A successful raid only truly feels complete once the Metagross is safely added to your collection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Mega Metagross worth raiding?

For most trainers, yes. It offers strong long-term value as a powerful Steel-type Mega Evolution and is expected to remain relevant in future raid rotations.

Can Mega Metagross be shiny?

If the shiny form is enabled during its raid rotation, trainers will have a chance to encounter a shiny Metagross after defeating the raid boss.

How many trainers are needed?

A trio should be achievable for prepared players with strong counters, while larger groups will find the raid much more comfortable.

What is Mega Metagross weak against?

Ground, Fire, Ghost, and Dark-type attacks deal super-effective damage and should form the core of your raid team.

Should I use Rare Candy on Metagross?

That depends on your resources, but many trainers consider Metagross one of the better Pokémon to invest in because of its usefulness across multiple areas of the game.

Is Mega Metagross useful after the raid event ends?

Absolutely. Its value extends well beyond the event itself, making it a Pokémon that many players will continue using for future raids.

Comparison With Other Steel-Type Mega Evolutions

Mega Metagross enters a category that already contains several Steel-type Mega Evolutions, but it brings a unique balance of strengths.

Mega Scizor offers respectable damage and good defensive utility, but its offensive ceiling generally doesn't match what Mega Metagross can achieve. Mega Aggron is incredibly bulky but often lacks the damage output that raid-focused players want. Mega Steelix can absorb damage effectively, yet it tends to play a more defensive role.

Mega Metagross stands out because it combines strong survivability with exceptional offensive potential. Rather than excelling in only one area, it performs at a high level across multiple categories.

For trainers who prioritize raid efficiency, Mega Metagross is likely to become the preferred Steel-type Mega Evolution in many situations.

Best Mega Evolutions to Bring

Choosing the right Mega Evolution can help both you and your teammates deal more damage throughout the raid.

Mega Gengar is an excellent choice if your group is using Ghost-type attackers. Its powerful attacks and team-wide Ghost boost can help shorten the battle significantly.

Mega Charizard Y is another strong option thanks to its ability to enhance Fire-type damage. This can be especially useful if several trainers are running Fire-based teams.

If available, Primal Groudon is arguably one of the most valuable choices because it boosts Ground-type attacks while also dealing tremendous damage itself. Since Ground-types are among the strongest counters to Mega Metagross, the extra support can benefit the entire raid group.

The best Mega Evolution isn't always the one that deals the most damage personally. Sometimes the greatest value comes from boosting the attacks of everyone around you.

Budget Counters for New Players

Not every trainer has a collection full of Shadow Pokémon, Mega Evolutions, or level 50 legendaries. Fortunately, Mega Metagross can still be challenged successfully using more accessible options.

Pokémon such as Excadrill, Chandelure, Gengar, Houndoom, and Garchomp can contribute meaningful damage without requiring extremely rare resources. Many of these Pokémon have appeared in events multiple times, making them realistic options for newer players.

The key is focusing on super-effective moves rather than simply using the highest-CP Pokémon in your storage. A moderately powered Ground or Fire-type attacker will usually outperform an unrelated legendary that only deals neutral damage.

New players should remember that teamwork matters just as much as individual counters. Even if your roster isn't perfect, contributing consistent damage alongside other trainers can still lead to a successful raid.

Discussions within the community usually focus on its future value rather than just the Mega Energy rewards. Many players see Mega Metagross as an investment that will continue paying off in future raids, especially whenever Fairy, Ice, or Rock-type bosses return.

Personal Raid Experience

Whenever a new Mega Raid boss arrives, I like to test different teams instead of relying on the same group of counters every time. What stood out to me about Mega Metagross is how durable it feels compared to many other Mega Raid bosses. Even when using strong super-effective attackers, its health doesn't disappear as quickly as you might expect.

Ground-type Pokémon consistently felt like the safest option during my battles. They provided reliable damage and usually survived long enough to avoid constant relobbies. Ghost-type attackers delivered excellent damage, but some of them could be knocked out surprisingly fast if Mega Metagross was using the right moves.

The raids felt comfortable with a well-prepared group, but smaller groups definitely benefited from having powered-up counters and Mega Evolutions active. Overall, Mega Metagross felt challenging enough to be interesting without becoming frustrating.

Will Mega Metagross Still Be Worth Using?

Some raid bosses are exciting for a few weeks and then slowly disappear from most players' teams. Mega Metagross doesn't feel like one of those Pokémon.

Its Steel typing gives it long-term relevance because Steel attackers are frequently useful against Fairy, Ice, and Rock-type raid bosses. Those raid targets appear regularly throughout the year, which means Mega Metagross is unlikely to spend much time sitting unused in storage.

What makes it even more appealing is the balance between damage and durability. Many top attackers hit hard but faint quickly. Mega Metagross is expected to offer both strong offensive pressure and enough bulk to stay on the field for extended periods.

If you're deciding whether to invest Mega Energy, Stardust, and XL Candy, Mega Metagross looks like the type of Pokémon that can remain valuable for years rather than months. For serious raiders, that kind of long-term usefulness is often worth the investment.

Common Mistakes Trainers Make Against Mega Metagross

One of the biggest mistakes I see is trainers relying on whatever high-CP Pokémon the game recommends. While the auto-selected team might look powerful, it often includes Pokémon that deal neutral damage instead of taking advantage of Mega Metagross's weaknesses.

Another common mistake is ignoring Ground-type attackers. Many players automatically choose Dragon-types because they are powered up and familiar, but strong Ground-types usually perform much better in this matchup.

I've also seen players forget to Mega Evolve before joining the raid. That extra damage boost may not seem huge, but when several trainers are battling together, those boosts add up quickly and can make the difference between a comfortable victory and a close finish.

Finally, some trainers enter the raid without reviving key counters from previous battles. Spending a minute preparing your team beforehand can have a bigger impact than most people realize.

Moveset Analysis: What Makes Some Mega Metagross Raids Harder?

Not every Mega Metagross raid feels exactly the same. The moveset it brings into battle can have a noticeable impact on how quickly your team performs and how often your Pokémon faint.

When Mega Metagross is using powerful Psychic-type attacks, Ghost and Dark-type counters can dish out impressive damage, but some may struggle to stay alive for long. On the other hand, Steel-type attacks can catch certain counters off guard and force more relobbies than expected.

In my experience, trainers often focus entirely on weaknesses and forget that movesets matter too. A raid that looks easy on paper can suddenly become much tougher if Mega Metagross happens to be carrying a move that hits your team particularly hard. Checking recommended counters before the battle starts can save a lot of healing items and frustration.

What Makes Mega Metagross Special?

Mega Metagross combines massive offensive power with impressive defensive stats. Its Steel/Psychic typing allows it to resist many common attack types, making it a difficult raid boss to defeat quickly.

For trainers who enjoy raids, gym battles, and PvE content, Mega Metagross is expected to be one of the best Steel-type Mega Evolutions available. Its high damage output can significantly boost raid performance against Fairy, Ice, and Rock-type Pokémon.

Unlike many Mega Evolutions that focus on either offense or defense, Mega Metagross excels at both, making it a highly desirable Mega Evolution for long-term use.

Mega Metagross Weaknesses

Understanding Mega Metagross's weaknesses is the key to building an effective raid team.

Mega Metagross is a Steel and Psychic-type Pokémon. Because of this combination, it takes super effective damage from:

  • Ground-type attacks
  • Ghost-type attacks
  • Fire-type attacks
  • Dark-type attacks

Ground-type attackers are often among the safest choices because they deal consistent damage while avoiding some of Mega Metagross's strongest resistances.

Ghost and Dark attackers can also perform extremely well, especially if the raid boss is using Psychic-type charged moves.

Trainers should avoid relying heavily on Normal, Grass, Poison, Psychic, Ice, Dragon, Fairy, Rock, or Steel attackers since Mega Metagross resists many of these types.

Best Mega Metagross Counters

Several Pokémon are expected to perform exceptionally well against Mega Metagross.

Primal Groudon

Primal Groudon stands out as one of the strongest counters thanks to its powerful Ground-type attacks. Its combination of bulk and damage allows it to remain on the battlefield for a long time while dealing consistent super-effective damage.

Shadow Tyranitar

Shadow Tyranitar's Dark-type attacks can deal heavy damage while benefiting from excellent overall durability.

Mega Blaziken

Mega Blaziken provides powerful Fire-type attacks and can support teammates through Mega boosts during the raid.

Can Mega Metagross Be Soloed?

Mega Metagross is expected to be one of the tougher Mega Raid bosses in Pokémon GO.

For most trainers, a solo victory is unrealistic due to the boss's enormous HP pool and defensive stats.

Duo

Trio

Most casual groups will find the raid comfortable with four or five trainers using decent counters.

Six or More Trainers

With six or more players, Mega Metagross should be defeated consistently without major difficulty.

Weather Boost Analysis

Weather can significantly influence raid difficulty and performance.

Sunny Weather

Sunny weather boosts Ground and Fire-type attacks. This is generally one of the best conditions for battling Mega Metagross because many top counters benefit from these boosts.

Foggy Weather

Foggy weather boosts Ghost and Dark-type attacks, making Pokémon such as Mega Gengar and Shadow Tyranitar even stronger.

Windy Weather

Windy weather boosts Psychic-type Pokémon. If Mega Metagross receives a weather boost, the raid may become slightly more challenging.

Snow Weather

Snow boosts Steel-type Pokémon, increasing Mega Metagross's effectiveness and survivability during the battle.
