What is Gigantamax Venusaur?

Gigantamax Venusaur is a special form of Venusaur introduced in Pokémon Sword and Shield. Unlike regular Dynamax Pokémon, Gigantamax forms change both appearance and their exclusive move.

When Venusaur Gigantamaxes, its flower grows into a massive canopy that releases toxic pollen across the battlefield, turning it into a long-duration pressure-based attacker in Max Battles.

G-Max Vine Lash (Signature Move)

  • G-Max Vine Lash is Venusaur’s exclusive Gigantamax move.
  • Deals Grass-type damage on impact.
  • Applies residual damage over time for several turns to all non-Grass opponents.
  • This makes it especially effective in longer raid battles where chip damage adds up.

Typing & Battle Overview

  • Type: Grass / Poison
  • Weak to: Fire, Flying, Ice, Psychic
  • Resists: Water, Electric, Grass, Fighting, Fairy

Venusaur doesn’t have extreme weaknesses, but Fire and Flying attackers are consistently the safest way to deal with it.

In Pokémon GO Raids

How it performs:

  • Acts as a bulky raid boss with sustained pressure rather than burst damage
  • Becomes more dangerous in long fights due to DoT effects
  • Difficulty scales heavily with group size and coordination

Weather Impact

  • Sunny / Clear: Boosts Venusaur’s Grass moves and makes raids harder
  • Windy: Helps Flying and Psychic counters perform better

Shiny Appearance

  • Normal Venusaur has a bright green body with a pink flower
  • Shiny Venusaur is darker with a slightly muted, yellow-toned flower
  • Gigantamax form makes the shiny difference more noticeable due to its large flower canopy

Why Trainers Care About It

  • It is part of the original starter Gigantamax lineup
  • Useful Grass-type attacker for raids when properly supported
  • Limited-time availability makes it a collection target
  • Performs well in group-based Max Battles

PvE vs PvP Use

PvE: Viable Grass attacker, but outclassed by top meta Megas and Shadow Pokémon.

PvP: Regular Venusaur is the competitive pick; Gigantamax form is mainly for raids and events.

Pokédex Insight

Venusaur converts sunlight into energy through its large flower, releasing it in powerful bursts of nature-based attacks when fully charged.

For most trainers, Gigantamax Venusaur is worth raiding at least a few times. While it isn't the strongest Grass-type attacker in Pokémon GO, it offers good collection value, a chance at a shiny encounter, and access to one of the original Gigantamax starter Pokémon.

Reasons to Raid Gigantamax Venusaur

  • Collection Value: Gigantamax Pokémon are usually available only during limited events, making them desirable additions to a collection.
  • Shiny Hunting: Trainers have a chance to encounter a shiny Venusaur after a successful raid.
  • Useful Grass-Type Attacker: Venusaur combines respectable damage with solid durability, making it useful against Water-, Ground-, and Rock-type raid bosses.
  • Event Experience: Gigantamax raids are often community-focused events that can be fun to tackle with larger groups.

Reasons You Might Skip It

  • Meta-Focused Players: If your goal is only to build the strongest raid teams, there are other Grass-type options that offer higher damage output.
  • Limited Local Community: Gigantamax raids generally require large groups, making them difficult for solo players or small communities.

Gigantamax Venusaur Raid Strategy

Gigantamax Venusaur is a challenging Max Battle that rewards preparation and teamwork. While strong counters are important, success often comes down to staying active throughout the battle and maintaining consistent damage pressure.

Build Your Team Around Its Weaknesses

Venusaur's Grass/Poison typing leaves it vulnerable to several attacking types. Fire-type Pokémon are usually the safest and most effective option, while Flying-, Psychic-, and Ice-type attackers can also perform well.

  • Fire-type attackers are generally the top choice.
  • Flying-type Pokémon provide reliable backup damage.
  • Psychic-type attackers can be useful in certain situations.
  • Avoid using Grass-type attackers because Venusaur strongly resists Grass-type moves.

Bring Enough Trainers

Smaller groups may struggle against Gigantamax Venusaur due to its durability and battle mechanics. Most groups will have a much easier time with 15 or more active trainers.

  • 10–15 trainers: Possible with strong teams and coordination.
  • 15–20 trainers: Comfortable for most groups.
  • 20+ trainers: Consistent and low-risk clears.

Focus on Survival as Well as Damage

Many raid failures happen because trainers focus entirely on attack power. A Pokémon that survives longer often contributes more total damage than one that faints quickly.

  • Use a balanced team rather than only fragile attackers.
  • Be prepared for longer battles.
  • Keep backup teams ready before entering the raid.

Pay Attention to Charged Attacks

Strong charged moves can quickly knock out attackers if ignored. Dodging important charged attacks can help keep your strongest Pokémon in battle longer and reduce the need for frequent relobbies.

Rejoin Quickly After Fainting

Time spent outside the battle means lost damage. Trainers who return quickly after their team faints contribute far more to the group's overall success.

  • Prepare battle parties in advance.
  • Keep healing items ready.
  • Re-enter the battle as soon as possible.

Quick Strategy Summary

  • Prioritize Fire-type attackers.
  • Bring a group of at least 15 trainers if possible.
  • Balance damage output with survivability.
  • Watch for powerful charged attacks.
  • Rejoin quickly whenever your team faints.

Sludge Bomb

  • Worth watching for when building raid teams.

Common Mistakes When Fighting Gigantamax Venusaur

Gigantamax Venusaur can be surprisingly difficult if your group isn't prepared. Many failed raids come down to a few common mistakes rather than a lack of trainers.

Using the Wrong Pokémon

One of the biggest mistakes is bringing Pokémon that do not take advantage of Venusaur's weaknesses. Grass-type attackers are especially ineffective because Venusaur resists Grass-type moves.

For the best results, focus on strong Fire-, Flying-, Psychic-, or Ice-type attackers whenever possible.

Ignoring Survivability

Many trainers build teams that focus entirely on damage. While high DPS is important, Pokémon that faint too quickly can actually reduce your overall contribution during longer battles.

A balanced team with both strong attackers and durable Pokémon is often more effective than a full lineup of glass cannons.

Treating It Like a Normal Raid

Gigantamax encounters are designed to be more demanding than standard raid battles. Smaller groups that easily defeat regular raid bosses may struggle if they underestimate the challenge.

If possible, join larger groups and coordinate with other trainers before the battle begins.

Slow Rejoins After Fainting

Time spent outside the battle is lost damage. Trainers who take too long to heal and rejoin can significantly reduce the group's chances of success.

  • Prepare battle parties in advance.
  • Keep healing items ready.
  • Re-enter the battle as quickly as possible.

Not Taking Advantage of Weather Boosts

Weather can have a noticeable impact on raid performance. Conditions that boost your counters can make the battle easier, while weather that strengthens Venusaur's attacks may increase the difficulty.

Checking the weather before a raid can help you choose the most effective team.

Lack of Team Coordination

Even strong groups can struggle if everyone uses random Pokémon. Coordinating counter types and entering the battle with prepared teams usually leads to faster and more consistent clears.

Quick Summary

  • Use Fire-, Flying-, Psychic-, or Ice-type counters.
  • Don't rely only on fragile attackers.
  • Expect a tougher battle than a standard raid.
  • Rejoin quickly after fainting.
  • Take advantage of weather and group coordination.
