What is Mega Glalie in Pokémon GO?

It is a Mega Raid Boss in Pokémon GO that players can battle to earn Mega Energy and useful raid rewards. After defeating it, you can:

  • Earn Mega Energy to Mega Evolve Glalie
  • Catch regular Glalie after the raid
  • Receive standard rewards like XP, Rare Candy, and Golden Razz Berries

Why Mega Glalie Is Important

Unlocks Ice-Type Mega Evolution

Defeating Mega Glalie gives you access to Mega Energy, which is required to Mega Evolve Glalie into its stronger form. This also provides long-term benefits such as:

  • Increased damage for Ice-type Pokémon in raids
  • Bonus Candy and XL Candy when catching Ice-types

Good Farming Opportunity

  • Relatively easy Mega Raid compared to top-tier bosses
  • Fast raid completion with proper counters
  • Helpful for building Ice-type raid teams

Not a Top Meta Mega

  • Outclassed by stronger Ice-type attackers in raids
  • Limited use in PvP battles
  • Main value is Mega Energy and collection progress

Difficulty Level

Overall, Mega Glalie is considered an easy to medium difficulty raid boss.

Why It’s Easy

  • Has multiple weaknesses (Fire, Fighting, Rock, and Steel)
  • Low defensive bulk compared to many Mega bosses
  • Can be defeated quickly with proper counters

What Makes It Slightly Challenging

  • Powerful Ice-type moves like Avalanche
  • Can punish poorly matched teams (especially Dragons)

Mega Glalie Raid Strategy Guide (Pokémon GO)

Mega Glalie raids can feel challenging at first, but with the right strategy and team setup, they become much easier. This guide explains how to approach the raid based on your player level and group size.

Can You Solo Mega Glalie?

Short answer: No, solo raids are not recommended.

Mega Glalie has a large raid HP pool, which makes solo attempts impractical even with strong counters.

  • Time limit is too short for solo completion
  • Even top attackers cannot finish in time
  • Ice-type moves can still deal heavy damage

Duo Strategy (2 Players)

A duo is possible for experienced trainers with strong counters and good coordination.

Recommended requirements:

  • Level 40+ trainers
  • High DPS counters with optimal movesets
  • Weather advantage (Sunny or neutral conditions preferred)

Best counters for duo raids:

  • Reshiram (Fire Spin + Fusion Flare)
  • Lucario (Counter + Aura Sphere)
  • Rampardos

Tips:

  • Dodge charged move Avalanche when possible
  • Re-enter quickly after fainting
  • Use Mega boosts like Mega Blaziken

Trio Strategy (3 Players)

Trio raids are much more comfortable and recommended for most players.

  • Level 30+ trainers are sufficient
  • No need for perfect IV Pokémon
  • Focus on type advantage over perfection

Good Pokémon choices:

  • Metagross
  • Conkeldurr
  • Darmanitan

Group Raids (4–6 Players)

This is the easiest and most recommended way to defeat Mega Glalie.

  • Fast and safe win condition
  • Minimal coordination required
  • Works even with mixed teams

Beginner Strategy

For new players, focus on survivability and basic type advantage.

  • Use Fire, Fighting, Rock, or Steel-type Pokémon
  • Avoid Dragons (they take heavy Ice damage)

Budget-friendly options:

  • Machamp
  • Flareon
  • Hariyama

Intermediate Strategy

At this level, focus on better movesets and team optimization.

  • Use proper PvE movesets (high DPS focus)
  • Include at least one Mega Pokémon for boost

Recommended Pokémon:

  • Blaziken
  • Chandelure

Advanced Strategy

Advanced players aim for high efficiency clears and faster raid completion.

  • Focus on maximum DPS output
  • Dodge only high-damage charged moves
  • Pre-build raid parties for quick re-entry

Top counters:

  • Shadow Machamp
  • Rampardos

Expert Strategy

Expert-level raids focus on duo clears and perfect optimization.

  • Level 50 Pokémon with XL investment
  • Best Friend raid bonus active
  • Perfect or near-perfect IV teams

Optimization tips:

  • Start with Mega boost Pokémon for team damage boost
  • Switch to highest DPS attackers afterward
  • Minimize relobby delays

Top meta picks:

  • Terrakion
  • Mega Blaziken
  • Reshiram

Common Mistakes

  • Using Dragon-types against Ice attacks
  • Ignoring Avalanche as a high-damage threat
  • Not using Mega boosts for team damage
  • Bringing bulky but low DPS Pokémon

How to Catch Mega Camerupt in Pokémon GO

In Pokémon GO, you don’t directly catch Mega Camerupt in its Mega form. Instead, you first defeat it in Mega Raids and then catch its base form, Camerupt. Mega Energy is used separately to unlock its Mega Evolution.

Your main goal during the raid is simple:

  • Defeat Mega Camerupt as quickly as possible
  • Earn as many Premier Balls as you can
  • Improve your throw accuracy for higher catch success

After winning a raid, the number of Premier Balls you receive depends on your performance and contribution.

Use Curveball Throws for Better Catch Rate

Curveball throws significantly improve your chances of catching Pokémon after raids.

  • Throw in a curved motion toward the target circle
  • Aim for consistent timing rather than rushing

Watch Attack Patterns Carefully

Mega Camerupt has slow and predictable attack animations, which makes timing easier.

  • Wait for the attack animation to finish
  • Throw immediately after the animation ends
  • This helps reduce missed throws caused by movement

Use the Circle Lock Technique

  • Hold the Poké Ball until the circle reaches a smaller size
  • Wait for an attack animation
  • Throw during the attack for better accuracy

Weather Boost – Mega Glalie

Weather Boost is a core Pokémon GO mechanic where real-world weather conditions influence Pokémon strength, spawns, and raid battles. It also affects how challenging raid bosses like Mega Glalie become.

Common Raid Mistakes in Mega Glalie Battles

Many players lose time or efficiency in Mega Glalie raids due to avoidable mistakes. Understanding these errors can help you win faster and use fewer resources.

Using Dragon-Type Pokémon

Mistake: Bringing strong Dragon-types such as Rayquaza or Garchomp.

Why it’s ineffective:

  • Ice-type attacks deal super effective damage to Dragons
  • Charged moves like Avalanche can knock them out quickly

Better approach: Use Fire, Fighting, Rock, or Steel-type counters instead.

Underestimating Avalanche

Mistake: Ignoring Mega Glalie’s charged move pressure.

Why it matters:

  • Avalanche is fast and hits hard
  • Can quickly reduce your team’s survivability

Fix: Dodge charged attacks when needed and use more durable Pokémon like Metagross.

Skipping Mega Evolution Support

Mistake: Not using any Mega Pokémon in the raid.

Why it matters:

  • Megas boost overall team damage
  • They also improve raid rewards like extra candy

Recommended Megas: Mega Blaziken or Mega Charizard Y

Relying Only on Auto-Selected Teams

Mistake: Using the recommended party without checking counters.

Why it fails:

  • Auto teams often prioritize bulk over effectiveness
  • They may not include optimal type advantages

Fix: Manually build your team using strong, super-effective attackers.

Low Damage Output Teams

Mistake: Using bulky Pokémon with low DPS.

Why it matters:

  • Raid timers require high damage output
  • Low DPS can lead to slow clears or failed raids

Delaying Re-Entry After Fainting

Why it matters:

  • Reduces total damage output
  • Slows down raid completion

Fix: Pre-build teams and rejoin immediately.

Ignoring Team Coordination in Small Groups

Mistake: Using random Pokémon in duo or trio raids.

  • Lack of synergy reduces overall damage efficiency

Fix: Coordinate roles such as Mega support + high DPS attackers.

Not Adjusting for Weather

  • Weather can boost both boss and attacker damage

Using Too Many Glass Cannons

Mistake: Building a team made only of fragile attackers.

Why it matters:

  • Frequent fainting leads to more downtime
  • Reduces overall raid efficiency

Fix: Balance your team with both high DPS and durable Pokémon.

Mistake: Not using dodge mechanics in duo or trio raids.

  • Fainting too early can cause raid failure
